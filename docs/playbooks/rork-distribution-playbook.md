# Rork distribution playbook — operational outline

Structured outline of the Rork team's published guide for taking a mobile app from launch to roughly $50k/month. Saved as decision-context for future product and distribution sessions on this repo. Source: the Rork team's distribution write-up; consult the original for full prose and tone. This file preserves the operational specifics — dollar figures, cadence, thresholds, formats, ordering — without reproducing the article verbatim.

---

## Step 1 — Pick the right app

- Commit to **one** app long enough to crack distribution. Replicate the system across more apps after the first one works.
- Two ways to pick what to build:
  - **(a)** Take a successful app and add your own twist (personality, colours, mascot). Plain copycats don't work.
  - **(b)** Combine the main features of two successful apps into one.
- Recommended for first-timers: **(a)**. Option (b) is harder.
- **Ship in under 2 weeks** from idea to App Store launch. Speed matters.
- **Launch-day conversion signal**: a soon-to-be-successful app gets **1–5 trials (or a direct conversion) on day one**. Apps that fail to convert often do so even after going viral on TikTok.
- **Pivot rule**: if no conversion on launch day, push for virality on social. **If you cross 100k views without a single conversion, drop the app and move to the next idea.**

## Step 2 — Obsess over distribution

- App = funnel. Top = distribution (views). Bottom = conversion. **Do not optimise anywhere below the top until distribution is moving.**
- Until distribution is running: no animation tuning, no mascot polish, no colour passes, no retention work, no new features, no onboarding optimisation. Wait until roughly **5k downloads** before touching the product.

### Four distribution channels — ordered by capital required (least to most)

#### 1. Make content yourself

- The recommended starting channel even if you have money. Builds your viral sense — required to evaluate the other channels later.
- **Cadence: 21 posts/day across TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube Shorts.** Mechanic: produce **7 originals**, cross-post to all three platforms.
- Research the formats your competitors are using — same hooks, same scripts, same CTAs.
- Three formats that work:

##### UGC Reaction + Demo
- Buy reaction clips (from friends or UGC creator marketplaces) and stitch your demo onto them.
- Demo length: **≤ 8 seconds**.
- The hook = the reaction clip + the on-screen text. Both have to land.
- Apps have hit **$10k+/month** on this format alone.

##### Slideshows
- Brand-consistent slideshow accounts with a clear identity and a direct download CTA.
- Bad pattern: Pinterest-style aesthetic dumps with no cohesion.
- Apps making **$20k+/month** are running this. Format also works as paid ad creative.

##### Memes
- Strong on Instagram. Mostly for brand awareness rather than direct conversion.
- Pair with a Manychat automation + Instagram Stories that drive downloads. Not a first format to test.

#### 2. Hire influencers

- Apps in the **$25k+/month** range commonly scale here.
- **Capital floor: ≥ $10k budget** (a lot of placements won't work — the budget covers the misses).
- Outreach volume: **≥ 100 DMs per day**.
- Open the DM with "Paid Promo Opportunity" (or equivalent) to cut through. Keep messages short and concrete.
- Filter:
  - Must be in your niche / speaking to your customers.
  - Strong engagement. Read the comments — fire emojis are noise; deep, specific replies are signal.
- Process rule: **never disclose the deal in DMs.** Get them on a call. If you can't, send the deal as a text to their phone number.
- Target rate: **$1 CPM** (cost per thousand views).
- Deal structure: start with a "trial" deal at **$1 CPM for 300k views with 40% upfront**, then move to **$1.2 CPM** ongoing.

#### 3. Hire UGC creators

- Considered the highest-leverage mobile distribution channel right now. Also the hardest to run.
- Not recommended for new builders without prior distribution experience or a co-founder. A UGC army is a full-time job — content direction, performance tracking, hiring, firing, paying.
- Outreach channel for UGC (not Instagram/email like influencers): **TikTok and UGC marketplaces**.
- Filter for: **2–3 existing viral videos, camera charisma, communication skills**. Follower count is not a signal.
- The skill compounds for life if you crack it.

#### 4. Paid ads

- Apps have gone from **$2.5k/month to $25k/month** on this channel.
- Common mistake: starting too early.
- **Prerequisites before turning ads on**:
  - At least **5 pieces of organic content already working** (your posts, influencer posts, or UGC).
  - Onboarding download-to-trial conversion **≥ 10%**.
- Use "organic ads": posts with a genuinely organic hook that feel like real organic videos, but with a clear and direct CTA. Avoid vague posts that try to convert through the comments.

## Step 3 — Obsess over onboarding

- **Trigger to start obsessing**: distribution is running at **≥ 300 daily downloads**.
- Onboarding = a three-act story:
  1. **Introduction** — paint the problem.
  2. **Climax** — the user experiences the app.
  3. **Conclusion** — prep for the paywall.
- Do not design from scratch. Study the best onboardings in your niche and outside it; adapt.

### Onboarding mechanics

- At least one **aha moment** that lands **within the first minute** — makes the user feel the weight of their problem.
- Onboarding questions exist to make the user reflect on their problem, **not for you to learn about them**. The reflection is the conversion mechanism.
- **Mirror the user's answers back** every few questions so the experience feels personalised.
- **Length sweet spot: 10–15 minutes.** Longer onboardings convert better as long as every screen adds value (sunk-cost effect at the paywall — people hate abandoning what they've invested in).
- **Let the user actually use the core feature inside onboarding.** Don't just describe it.
- **Show the review modal right after the user completes the core feature**, when they're at peak excitement. Most users won't pay but many will leave a review. Reviews compound into organic downloads.

### Onboarding exit threshold

- Keep iterating onboarding until **download-to-trial conversion ≥ 10%** before moving to the next step.

## Step 4 — Scale and iterate the product

- Pre-conditions: good app + strong onboarding + working distribution system.
- Scale = more ad spend, more UGC creators, more influencers, more self-made content.
- The product can now be touched. Constraints:
  - Be deliberate. Only add features with data behind them.
  - Don't bloat with features on a whim.
  - **Optimise toward revenue events** (e.g. trial-to-conversion rate), not toward feature counts.
- Time allocation at this stage: **90–100% on marketing**.

## Anchor numbers — quick reference

| Number | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **< 2 weeks** | Idea-to-App-Store target |
| **1–5 trials** | Launch-day conversion signal (a "soon-to-be-successful" app) |
| **100k views** | Pivot threshold without a conversion — drop the app |
| **21 posts/day** | Self-made content cadence (7 originals × 3 platforms) |
| **100 DMs/day** | Influencer outreach volume |
| **$10k** | Minimum budget floor before running influencers |
| **$1 CPM → $1.2 CPM** | Influencer rate progression (trial → ongoing) |
| **300k views, 40% upfront** | Influencer first-deal structure |
| **300 daily downloads** | Threshold to start obsessing over onboarding |
| **10–15 minutes** | Onboarding length sweet spot |
| **≥ 10%** | Onboarding download-to-trial conversion to start paid ads |
| **≥ 5 organic pieces** | Minimum working organic content before paid ads |
| **90–100%** | Marketing time allocation at scale |

---

## Channel selection by capital available

- **$0** → self-made content (TikTok / Instagram / YouTube Shorts, 21 posts/day).
- **$10k+** → influencers (in addition to self-made).
- **UGC creators** → add when you have prior distribution experience or a co-founder to share the load.
- **Paid ads** → only after ≥5 working organic pieces + onboarding ≥10% conversion + ≥300 daily downloads.
